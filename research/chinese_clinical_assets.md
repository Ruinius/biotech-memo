# Chinese Clinical Assets Brought to Western Markets — Research Profile

## Overview

Chinese biotechs have undergone a fundamental transformation from licensing Western drugs *in* to licensing Chinese-origin drugs *out*. In 2025, Chinese companies signed a record **$135.7 billion** in outbound licensing agreements — representing **one-third of all global licensing spend**. China-sourced drug candidates will make up almost **40% of all licensing deals in 2025**, up from less than 3% five years ago. This marks the year China became the world's most important source of licensable drug assets.

**Source:** [Axios — China pharma deals threaten U.S. biotech (Sep 2025)](https://www.axios.com/2025/09/08/china-deals-biotech-us) | [Vision Lifesciences — China Biotech Outbound Licensing Tracker 2026](https://visionlifesciences.com/insights/china-biotech-outbound-licensing-tracker)

## The Volume of Chinese Clinical Assets

### IND Filings and Regulatory Throughput

China's National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) has undergone one of the fastest regulatory modernizations in pharmaceutical history:

- **2023:** NMPA accepted **2,298 innovative drug IND applications**, with **1,918 approved** (approval rate >80%)
- By comparison, the U.S. FDA typically receives about **1,500 new IND submissions annually**
- In 2024, NMPA approved over **110 innovative drugs** (domestic and imported), up 11.1% year-over-year
- In 2024, **48 first-in-class innovative drugs** were approved, vs. 21 in 2022
- Rare disease approvals leapt from 3 (2022) to 45 (2023) to 55 (2024)
- Since 2020, nearly **496 NDAs have entered Priority Review** (42.5% for oncology)
- In 2025, NMPA proposed reducing IND review times from 60 to **30 working days**, matching the FDA's standard

**Source:** [PMC — Current landscape of innovative drug development and regulatory support in China (2025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12280122) | [KPMG — China Life Sciences Sector Overview and Outlook (2025)](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/cn/pdf/en/2025/04/china-life-sciences-sector-overview-and-outlook.pdf) | [IntuitionLabs — China NMPA Drug Approval Pathways](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/china-nmpa-drug-approval-pathways)

## The Innovation Arbitrage

Chinese drug discovery costs are **30-40% lower** than in the US/EU, while clinical trial enrollment is **2-3x faster** due to China's massive patient population. This "innovation arbitrage" is the fundamental driver: Western pharma can access de-risked, clinical-stage assets at valuations that still represent significant upside versus internal development.

**Source:** [Vision Lifesciences](https://visionlifesciences.com/insights/china-biotech-outbound-licensing-tracker)

## Notable Successes

### Legend Biotech / J&J — CARVYKTI (ciltacabtagene autoleucel)
- Chinese-origin CAR-T cell therapy for multiple myeloma
- Licensed to J&J; became the first Chinese-origin CAR-T to receive FDA approval
- Generated **$1+ billion** in revenue in its first full year on market
- Validated the model of Chinese clinical assets achieving Western regulatory approval and commercial success

### BeiGene / Novartis — Tislelizumab (PD-1 inhibitor)
- Landmark 2021 deal: BeiGene licensed tislelizumab to Novartis for **$2.2 billion**
- Helped establish the "China-to-global" licensing model
- FDA approval granted in 2023 for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma

### Akeso / Summit — Ivonescimab (AK112)

- $5.0 billion deal with Summit Therapeutics

## Data Integrity: The Shadow Side

### The 2015 CFDA Self-Examination Scandal

In July 2015, the Chinese FDA ordered companies to conduct self-examination of clinical trial data behind **1,622 drug applications**. Companies were given the option to submit a self-report for authority inspection or voluntarily withdraw applications without penalty.

**Result:** About **80% of applications were voluntarily pulled**. While the CFDA later disputed the characterization that "80% of China's clinical trial data are fraudulent," even they admitted the investigation revealed serious issues with clinical research quality, including:
- Deliberate data fabrication
- Non-compliance with Good Clinical Practice (GCP)
- Incomplete data
- Failure to demonstrate safety and effectiveness

**27 applications and 11 clinical trial institutions** and CROs suspected of providing fraudulent data were investigated.

**Source:** [Fierce Biotech — Dishonest participants, another chapter of China's fake data story](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/cro/dishonest-participants-another-chapter-china-s-clintrial-fake-data-story) | [Fierce Pharma — CFDA disputes claim 80% of Chinese trials faked data](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma-asia/cfda-disputes-claim-80-chinese-clinical-trials-were-faked) | [BMJ 2016;355:i5396](https://www.bmj.com/content/355/bmj.i5396)

### The Zheng Xiaoyu Execution (2007)

In 2007, the Chinese Government executed Zheng Xiaoyu, the former head of China's drug regulator (then the State Food and Drug Administration), for accepting bribes in exchange for issuing licences to untested medicines. Drugs approved during Zheng's term had deadly consequences, including a Chinese-made cough syrup that killed around 40 people in Panama due to diethylene glycol incorrectly labelled as harmless glycerine.

**Source:** [Pharmaceutical Technology — Tackling false trial data in China](https://www.pharmaceutical-technology.com/features/featuretackling-false-trial-data-in-china-5691656)

### Ongoing FDA Concerns

FDA officials, including Dr. Richard Pazdur, have expressed concerns about China-based clinical trial data:

> "When drugs are tested only or primarily in 1 country such as China, it is difficult to assess whether the drug would have the same benefits and safety profile in the US population."

Pazdur also expressed concern about potentially outdated study designs and data integrity, citing the 2016 BMJ analysis.

In 2025, the FDA issued General Correspondence Letters to **two Chinese third-party testing companies** after discovering data that was falsified or otherwise found to be invalid. FDA Commissioner Marty Makary stated: "The FDA has no room for bad actors."

**Source:** [Blood Cancers Today — FDA Expresses Concerns Over China-Based Clinical Trial Data](https://www.bloodcancerstoday.com/post/fda-expresses-concerns-over-china-based-clinical-trial-data) | [FDA Press Announcement (2025)](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-takes-action-address-data-integrity-concerns-two-chinese-third-party-testing-firms)

### Quality Improvement Since 2015

China's clinical trial quality has improved significantly:
- 2003: China GCP guidelines released, identifying subject protection and data integrity as basic principles
- 2015: Four rigorous management policies implemented requiring sponsors to re-evaluate authenticity, integrity, and compliance
- China's ICH membership (2017) allowed integration of Chinese clinical data into global drug programs
- Top Chinese biotechs now use **global CROs** (IQVIA, Parexel, Covance) for pivotal trials
- NMPA inspection processes increasingly consistent with FDA/EMA standards

**Source:** [PMC — Unveiling quality of clinical trial in China (2024)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11110953)

## Five Hidden Contractual Traps in China Licensing Deals

Morgan Lewis (April 2026) identified five critical contractual traps that erode value in Chinese biotech cross-border deals:

### 1. Toothless Anti-Shelving Clauses
It is not uncommon for an MNC to acquire an asset only to prioritize a competing internal pipeline. While contracts often include Commercially Reasonable Efforts (CRE) clauses, these are **notoriously difficult to enforce**. Innovators must demand specific, measurable development milestones and hard deadlines.

### 2. Change-of-Control Vulnerabilities
If the licensee is acquired by a competitor of the Chinese innovator, the asset's future is jeopardized. The contract must explicitly dictate how licenses and data rights will be handled in a change-of-control scenario.

### 3. "Disappearing" Milestones
Licensees may attempt to restructure clinical development plans post-closing, **technically bypassing the conditions that trigger milestone payments**. Precise definitions of what constitutes a triggering event are essential.

### 4. Ambiguous Data Rights
Clashes over the ownership and use of clinical data generated during the collaboration is a **common battleground**. Innovators must ensure they retain sufficient rights to leverage global trial data to support their domestic regulatory filings.

### 5. Data Integrity and Compliance
MNCs demand rigorous validation that Chinese clinical data meets FDA/EMA standards. They will heavily scrutinize a licensor's compliance with **China's cross-border data transfer regulations**, ensuring that global development will not be hindered by data localization mandates. Particularly in AI-driven drug discovery deals, MNCs demand absolute clarity on the ownership of AI training data and the patentability of AI-generated drugs.

**Source:** [Morgan Lewis — A Strategic Playbook for Chinese Biotech Cross-Border Deals (Apr 2026)](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/04/a-strategic-playbook-for-chinese-biotech-cross-border-deals-navigating-the-new-value-paradigm)

## Additional Pitfalls of Licensing Chinese Assets

### Cultural and Governance Friction
Freshfields Bruckhaus Deringer notes that MNCs experienced with U.S./European biotechs may be surprised by Chinese biopharma companies' priorities:
- **Increased focus on payment terms and upfront payments** — Certainty of immediate investment is viewed as validation of underlying technology
- **Less focus on milestones, royalties, and termination consequences** — Fewer objections to diligence or reversion obligations
- **Governing law and dispute resolution** take outsized importance given geopolitical tensions

**Source:** [Freshfields — Novel Considerations in Licensing Deals with Chinese Biotechs](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/a-fresh-take/novel-considerations-in-licensing-deals-with-chinese-biotechs-102l4pd)

### IP and Manufacturing Transfer
- Chinese patent quality has improved dramatically, but composition-of-matter claims, antibody sequence patents, and manufacturing process patents need careful review
- The most common gap: assessing whether the **manufacturing process can be transferred to a non-Chinese CDMO** if needed
- BIOSECURE Act does not affect drug licensing agreements, technology transfer, or IP licensing — but manufacturing by designated Chinese CDMOs is affected
- The critical diligence question: "Can this drug be manufactured outside of China for US/EU commercial supply?" If yes (and it usually is), BIOSECURE is not a deal-breaker

**Source:** [Vision Lifesciences](https://visionlifesciences.com/insights/china-biotech-outbound-licensing-tracker) | [Drug Patent Watch — China's Pharmaceutical Innovation Ecosystem](https://www.drugpatentwatch.com/blog/obstacles-opportunities-chinese-pharmaceutical-innovation)

## Therapeutic Area Dominance

### ADCs (Antibody-Drug Conjugates)
- Chinese biotechs account for **~90% of all global ADC licensing activity**
- Key deals: CSPC-AZ, Kelun-Merck, RemeGen, SystImmune
- This reflects China's structural advantages in protein engineering, linker-payload chemistry, and clinical trial speed

### Bispecific Antibodies
- Chinese companies hold **~50% of global deals**
- PD-1/VEGF bispecifics are the hottest space; **7 of 9 clinical candidates originate from China**
- Key deals: Akeso-Summit, Biotheus-BioNTech, Hengrui-Merck KGaA

### Small Molecules (Next-Gen)
- Growing rapidly beyond biologics
- Key deals: Hengrui-GSK, various KRAS/CDK4 programs

### Cell & Gene Therapy
- Emerging but accelerating
- Key deals: Legend-J&J (CARVYKTI), Gracell-AbbVie

## The NewCo Licensing Model

A notable 2025 trend: NewCo licensing models are increasingly used for Chinese outbound deals. Rather than licensing to established pharma, Chinese biotechs partner with VC-backed NewCos that offer:

- **Higher economics:** Chinese licensors get significant equity stakes + upfront + milestone payments
- **Faster decision-making:** 3-6 months vs. 9-18 months with large pharma
- **Asset focus:** 100% focus on the licensed asset vs. competing within a large portfolio
- **Built-to-buy:** End game is acquisition by Big Pharma at a premium

**Case Study:** Hengrui's $1.1B deal with Braveheart Bio (a Forbion/OrbiMed-backed NewCo) for cardiomyopathy drug HRS-1893.

**Source:** [Vision Lifesciences](https://visionlifesciences.com/insights/china-biotech-outbound-licensing-tracker)

## Implications for Biotech Strategy

1. **Any pharma BD team not actively sourcing assets from China is missing the single largest pool of innovative drug candidates in the world.**
2. **ADC dominance** — China's ~90% share of global ADC licensing means building an ADC portfolio without Chinese sourcing creates a structural disadvantage.
3. **Bispecific antibody opportunity** — 7 of 9 clinical PD-1/VEGF bispecific candidates originate from China; this is the hottest space in oncology.
4. **Data integrity remains a real risk** — The 2015 scandal and ongoing FDA concerns require rigorous due diligence on CRO partnerships, site monitoring, and GCP compliance.
5. **Contractual traps can erode deal value** — Anti-shelving clauses, change-of-control provisions, milestone definitions, and data rights must be negotiated with extreme precision.
6. **BIOSECURE is navigable** — Most licensing deals fall outside the Act's scope, but manufacturing transfer planning is essential.
7. **Regulatory sophistication is increasing** — Some Chinese clinical designs now support global registrations from the outset.
8. **China is no longer cheap** — Average upfront values have tripled from $52M to $172M (2022-2026); the arbitrage is in speed and volume, not just cost.
